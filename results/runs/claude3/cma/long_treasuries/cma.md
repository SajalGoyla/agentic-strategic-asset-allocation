## Judgment

**Regime logic.** The stage-1 call is a fragile, late-cycle-adjacent expansion with sticky above-history inflation, held at low confidence (0.35) and with a 20-30% recession probability. For the longest-duration nominal asset in the universe the regime label matters less than which macro surprise arrives over three years. A deflationary growth shock would drive a large, convexity-boosted gain. A renewed inflation surprise, which the -0.27 inflation score warns of, would drive a large loss, since roughly 16-17 years of duration turns 100bp into about 16% of price. These two tails roughly offset, so the regime gives no clean directional tilt. I therefore do not lean on regime_adjusted. Its 8.23% comes from the three years after past expansion months, and those were overwhelmingly drawn from the 1990-2020 secular fall in yields, so it measures a tailwind that started from much higher yields and cannot repeat in the same form. Exhibit 4's late-cycle guidance points toward valuation-anchored methods, and for a bond that means the starting yield.

**Valuation context.** Valuation is the strongest input here. The 20-30 year yield averages 5.66%, and the DGS30 yield-level signal is +0.79 against its own history, which makes long Treasuries cheap by their own standards. The term-structure split is the key judgment (consideration 4). The starting yield sits about 1.6pp above the 4.03% T-bill. If the expected average short rate over the bonds' life is roughly 3.5-4%, the implied term premium is about +1.5-2%. That is high relative to the near-zero or negative estimates of the past decade, and it is what separates this sleeve from intermediate Treasuries. I assume only part of that premium is earned over the next three years. The 3-year horizon is far shorter than the bonds' duration, so the starting yield is a noisy predictor here: a modest further rise in yields from sticky inflation could absorb a year or more of carry. That is why I shade below the 6.43% building block rather than taking it whole. Credit loss is correctly zero, and the 0.77pp variance add-back converts the yield to the arithmetic convention. Cheap valuation also argues against the low end of the range: bl_equilibrium's +0.16% excess return is a CAPM artifact, because Treasuries hedge the equity-heavy ETF market portfolio. It is not a valuation view.

**Signal alignment.** The signals split cleanly by category. Valuation (yield_level +0.79) and mean reversion (+0.23, after a deeply negative 36-month run) support the yield-based estimate. Trend (-0.56), 12-1 momentum (-0.28) and relative momentum (-0.89, near the bottom of the 18 assets) say yields are still rising and the sleeve is losing ground. The macro alignment signals are essentially zero; the measured correlations with the dimension scores are tiny. The composite is +0.18, mildly bullish. I read this as confirming the direction of the building block, a positive expected excess return from a high starting yield. The adverse momentum justifies hedging the magnitude, which is why 20% goes to the lower equilibrium anchor. I do not set the estimate at the bottom of the range. No fund-flow or positioning view is claimed, because this pipeline does not source one.

**Rationale.** For a default-free bond, the starting yield is the strongest single predictor, so yield_building_block (6.43%, confidence 0.80) carries 80% of the weight. The other 20% goes to bl_equilibrium (4.19%). This is not because equilibrium is a good forecast for a hedge asset. It is a deliberate haircut for horizon mismatch: three years against roughly 16-17 years of duration leaves substantial mark-to-market risk if sticky inflation pushes yields higher, and price trend and momentum still point that way. The result is 0.8 x 6.43 + 0.2 x 4.19 = 5.98%. That is about 1.95pp over the 4.03% T-bill, which implies about a 1.5pp term premium earned, a Sharpe of about 0.16 at 12.43% volatility, and a value comfortably inside [4.19, 8.23]. I gave zero weight to historical_erp (6.98%) and regime_adjusted (8.23%). Both are built on 1990-2026 returns dominated by the secular decline in yields, and the 5- and 10-year windows of -8.5% and -2.8% a year show how regime-specific that history was. Auto_blend is excluded to avoid double-counting its components and to keep those history-based methods out. Two risks fall outside the point estimate. First, this 'safe' sleeve has suffered drawdowns near -48% (consideration 2), well beyond the -25% IPS limit, so position sizing must respect that. Second, convexity's asymmetric payoff in a deflationary shock (consideration 3) is real tail-hedge value that the covariance matrix does not capture. Governing policy is config/ips.yaml, which is ratified.
