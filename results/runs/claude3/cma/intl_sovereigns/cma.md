## Judgment

**Regime logic.** Stage 1 calls a fragile, late-cycle-adjacent expansion at low confidence (0.35), with a 20-30% recession probability. Exhibit 4 tilts late-cycle judgments toward regime-adjusted and valuation-anchored methods and away from long-run averages. For this asset that means the starting-yield builder plus the regime-adjusted candidate. After past expansion months, unhedged ex-US sovereigns returned -0.84% a year in excess of T-bills over the next three years, consistent with a strong-dollar expansion pattern. Its credibility weight is a healthy 0.72 on 155 months, but the 0.25 confidence and the low-confidence regime call argue for only a minority weight (20%). In the recession branch, foreign duration would rally. The USD is a typical haven in a US-led slowdown, though, so the currency leg could offset local bond gains. Regime logic therefore does not justify moving far from the yield anchor in either direction.

**Valuation context.** The valuation lens is local long yields. The five-market mean (Germany, Japan, UK, France, Canada) is 3.64%, and the yield_level signal (+0.45) says this is high against the index's own post-2014 history. That makes the carry starting point the best single predictor of the local-currency return over a 3-year horizon near duration. Key consideration 2 applies directly: the 1990-2026 history was earned through a secular fall in yields and a decade of zero or negative JGB and Bund yields. That pattern cannot repeat from here in the same form, so historical_erp (5.32%, built on a 1.29% average excess return) gets zero weight despite its 0.30 confidence. The local yield sits about 40bp below the 4.03% US T-bill, so the asset carries negative carry against cash before any currency move. Per consideration 4, this is the unhedged index. A hedged version would pick up the US-foreign short-rate differential as hedge carry and run at far lower volatility. I have not mixed hedged evidence into this estimate. The 0.36pp variance add-back in the yield builder uses the full 8.45% unhedged volatility, which is mostly currency.

**Signal alignment.** The composite signal is roughly neutral at +0.03, but it splits sharply by category. Valuation is supportive (yield_level +0.45). Technicals are clearly negative: trend -0.43, 12-1 momentum -0.27, and relative momentum -0.78, near the bottom of the 18 asset classes. The 1-year return of -5.73% and 5-year return of -4.22% are largely dollar-driven. The macro alignment signals are all near zero, confirming that local-rate and growth factors explain little of this asset's USD return. The negative technicals and the negative regime_fit (-0.06) argue against leaning on the higher BL or historical figures. The supportive yield signal argues against going to the regime-adjusted floor. Both point toward a final number modestly above the yield builder and below the auto-blend, so the estimate sits between the anchor and the blend.

**Rationale.** Selected a custom blend: 60% yield_building_block (4.00%), 20% regime_adjusted (3.55%), 20% bl_equilibrium (5.11%), and 0% historical_erp. The result is 4.13%, within the [3.55, 5.32] range and 38bp below the 4.51% auto-blend. The auto-blend gave 21% to historical_erp, which key consideration 2 rules out because the falling-yield, zero-rate history cannot repeat; I removed that weight entirely. The yield builder is the anchor per the fixed-income guidance. Per consideration 1, though, this is a currency position wearing a bond label: exchange rates supply most of the 8.45% volatility, and the yield builder does not forecast them. My explicit dollar assumption is a flat trade-weighted USD over the 3-year horizon (zero expected FX return). I neither assume uncovered interest parity, which would credit foreign currencies with appreciating by the roughly 40bp carry shortfall, nor extrapolate the recent dollar strength the momentum signals reflect. BL keeps a 20% weight because it is the only candidate that prices this asset's covariance with the broader portfolio. Its 0.08% market weight makes its +1.08% premium thinly grounded, so it does not get its full confidence share. Per consideration 3, the result is the weakest risk-adjusted profile in the universe. At 4.13% against a 4.03% T-bill and 8.45% volatility, the ex-ante Sharpe is about 0.01, below even the roughly 0.05 the auto-blend would imply. That near-zero compensation for currency risk should be stated plainly to portfolio construction. Per consideration 5, the currency leg overlaps intl_corporates, so the two line items diversify each other less than they appear to. Confidence is low: the dispersion is tight at 1.77pp, but the dominant risk driver, the dollar, is unforecast by every candidate.
