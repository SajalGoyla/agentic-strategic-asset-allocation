## Judgment

**Regime logic.** Stage 1 labels the regime an expansion, but a fragile, late-cycle-adjacent one with sticky above-history inflation. Its confidence is low (0.35) and recession probability is 20-30%. Exhibit 4 tilts late-cycle environments toward valuation and regime-adjusted methods. Here the regime-adjusted candidate (12.83%) is conditioned on the 'expansion' label. That label is the least certain input in the regime view, and its credibility weight (0.72) was set without knowing the label is borderline late-cycle. The forward-looking 3-year excess after expansion months (+8.98%) is barely above the unconditional +8.34%, so the label adds little information while carrying the full 1990-2026 sample's multiple expansion. I therefore cut regime_adjusted to 5% rather than raising it. I also tilt toward the valuation methods (40% combined), which is the late-cycle half of the Exhibit 4 rule. Financial conditions (+0.35) are the only clearly supportive dimension. That supports keeping a meaningful equilibrium and survey anchor rather than going fully to the valuation floor. Negative monetary-policy and inflation scores hurt this long-duration, tech-heavy sleeve, which sells off on rising real yields (consideration 3).

**Valuation context.** CAPE at 40.3 is extreme even against the post-1990 median of 26.4. The real earnings yield is only 2.48%. So valuations are stretched, and Exhibit 4 directs weight toward implied_erp_cape and inverse_gordon. The Gordon build uses net payout, 0.93% dividends plus 1.28% net buybacks, which addresses consideration 4. A dividend-only yield would understate the income return by over a percentage point, so no further adjustment is needed. Its low headline (3.57%) comes from the -4.14%/yr valuation-reversion term, which assumes CAPE returns to 26.4 over 10 years. Mean reversion is real but slow, and the horizon here is 3 years, so I do not let this candidate dominate. I cap it at 20% rather than its 21% blend share, and balance it with the CAPE earnings-yield method (5.87%), which takes no reversion. The historical ERP (12.67%) embeds a 1990-2026 period of rising multiples. That is the very valuation expansion the CAPE signal says cannot repeat from here, so it is cut to 10%. Concentration (consideration 2) matters here. With the top ten names at about a third of the index, the aggregate CAPE is driven by a few mega-cap growth firms. Index returns therefore hinge on whether their earnings justify the multiples, which raises the downside tail.

**Signal alignment.** The composite signal is -0.25, and the bearish weight is valuation-driven. CAPE scores -0.78 and the 36-month mean-reversion signal scores -0.60 after a 22.8% annualised 3-year run. Short-horizon technicals are mildly positive: momentum +0.17, trend +0.14 and relative momentum +0.33. Regime fit is +0.23. So trend has not yet turned, but forward-return indicators point below history. This confirms leaning below the 8.24% auto-blend and well below the historical methods. The positive momentum and financial-conditions readings argue against going to the valuation floor (3.57-5.87%). Dollar exposure (consideration 5) is treated as an earnings headwind, not a return sign: the index's return can hold up on safe-haven dollar rallies, so I make no separate adjustment. No fund-flow or positioning view is used, because this pipeline cannot source one.

**Rationale.** I set a custom blend at 7.47%, 0.77pp below the auto-blend. The blend weights the survey consensus at 25% (SPF STOCK10, a fresh independent outside view) and Black-Litterman equilibrium at 20%. The two valuation methods get 20% each, and the backward-looking historical ERP and regime methods only 10% and 5%. The auto-blend weights candidates by stated confidence and takes no view on whether their assumptions hold now. The historical and regime methods assume the 1990-2026 multiple expansion persists, which is implausible from a CAPE of 40. The Gordon method assumes a full 10-year reversion that a 3-year horizon will capture only partly. The result sits inside the [3.57, 12.83] range. It implies an excess return of about 3.4% over the 4.03% T-bill and a Sharpe of about 0.23 at 14.76% volatility, modest and consistent with stretched valuations. The S&P 500 is 60% of the IPS benchmark, so this number drives every tracking-error figure (consideration 1). For that reason I keep the departure from the blend moderate and well justified rather than taking the valuation floor. Concentration in a few tech and AI mega-caps and sensitivity to real yields widen the outcome distribution, so confidence is low given 9.3pp of dispersion and a low-confidence regime call. Governing policy is config/ips.yaml, whose status is ratified.
