## Judgment

**Regime logic.** Stage 1 calls a fragile, late-cycle-adjacent expansion with sticky inflation, at low confidence (0.35), and puts recession probability at 20-30%. For IG corporates this argues against the regime_adjusted candidate. Its 6.65% comes from the three years after past expansion months, and those forward windows sit inside a 1990-2026 sample of almost continuously falling yields. It also gives no weight to the late-cycle tail, where spreads widen. The regime-fit signal is only +0.06, so the expansion label adds little information for this asset. The tight-but-fragile backdrop does matter for the spread leg. Loose financial conditions (+0.35) point to spreads nearer the tight end of their range. A 20-30% recession probability over a 3-year horizon means a real chance of spread widening and mark-to-market losses on an asset with roughly 8-year duration. I therefore haircut modestly below the starting yield rather than above it, and give regime_adjusted zero weight.

**Valuation context.** Key consideration 1 says to decompose the estimate. Most of the ~6.0% starting yield (BAMLC0A0CMEY) is the Treasury leg; the credit spread is a minority of it. The yield_level signal (+0.46 on BAA) says the all-in yield is attractive against its own history, but that comes mainly from where Treasury yields are, not from wide spreads. The view expressed here is about the spread leg. Consideration 4 applies: spreads mean-revert, and with financial conditions loose the starting spread is not where the forward return is. Upside from further tightening is capped, while widening risk stays intact. Consideration 2 also applies. The 0.10pp expected credit loss in yield_building_block is appropriately small for IG, and the spread already prices default risk, so I do not subtract any further loss. Doing so would count it twice. The haircut comes instead from horizon mismatch and spread asymmetry: a 3-year horizon is well short of ~8-year duration, so the yield is a good but imperfect predictor. Consideration 3 also applies. BBB issuance is now roughly half the index, so the historical excess return of +2.68% was earned on a higher-quality index during secular yield decline. That flatters historical_erp (6.71%) and I give it zero weight.

**Signal alignment.** The composite is close to neutral (+0.04), but its parts disagree. Valuation is clearly positive (yield level +0.46). The technicals are clearly negative: trend -0.65, relative momentum -0.67, 12-1 momentum -0.32. They are the mirror image of the same fact. The 1-year return of -4.03% is what pushed the starting yield up, so poor recent price action and a higher forward yield are one event seen twice. For bonds over a multi-year horizon the starting yield is the dominant predictor, and price momentum has little forward value. I therefore do not cut the estimate further to chase the technicals. The macro alignment signals are all within ±0.06 and do not move the view. Overall the signals neither confirm a bullish departure above the yield nor justify a large cut; they support staying close to yield_building_block with a mild downward hedge.

**Rationale.** For IG corporates at a horizon near duration, yield_building_block (6.17%: 6.02% yield, minus 0.10% credit loss, plus 0.25% variance add-back) is the strongest single candidate. It gets 80% of the weight, more than the 46% the auto-blend gives it. The remaining 20% goes to bl_equilibrium (5.01%). It is a cross-check that does not depend on the falling-yield history. It also expresses the departure from the yield-based figure, which is deliberate and has three reasons. First, spreads are likely near the tight end, so the outcome is asymmetric (consideration 4). Second, a 3-year horizon against ~8-year duration leaves meaningful mark-to-market exposure. Third, recession probability of 20-30% raises the chance of spread widening. historical_erp and regime_adjusted get zero weight. Both rest on a 1990-2026 sample of secular yield decline and on a higher-quality index than today's BBB-heavy one (consideration 3), so both overstate the forward return. The result is 0.8×6.17 + 0.2×5.01 = 5.94%, within the 5.01-6.71% range and about 0.2pp below the starting-yield figure. Dispersion is tight (1.70pp), so the choice moves the estimate only slightly. For portfolio construction, consideration 5 matters: correlation with US large cap is 0.58 and rises in stress, so this 5.94% should not be read as a Treasury-like equity hedge.
