## Judgment

**Regime logic.** For cash the regime matters only through the expected policy-rate path over the next three years; there is no premium to tilt. The stage-1 view is a fragile, late-cycle-adjacent expansion, classified at low confidence (0.35), with a 20-30% recession probability. Inflation is sticky and above its history, and the monetary-policy score is mildly negative (-0.22). This skews the 3-year path asymmetrically. Sticky inflation limits how far and how fast the Fed can cut in the base case. If the fragile expansion breaks, though, cuts would be fast and deep and would pull the average bill rate well below today's 4.03%. Quoting the spot yield for three years implicitly assumes no cuts at all, and that is the tail the regime view makes least likely. The regime_adjusted candidate does not capture this. Its -0.10% regime excess return is just the spot bill rate restated, because cash's excess return over T-bills is roughly zero by construction.

**Valuation context.** The valuation lens for cash is the policy path (DTB3, DGS1MO, DFF), and the build-up is the expected average overnight rate over the horizon (Key consideration 2). The starting DTB3 of 4.03% is high against its own history, which the yield_level signal of +0.57 reflects. The SPF median BILL10 of 3.00% is the professional consensus for the average bill rate over ten years, so it embeds an eventual decline toward a neutral rate. Three of the five available candidates add essentially no information. historical_erp and regime_adjusted (-0.10pp excess) and bl_equilibrium (+0.00pp at a 2.8% weight) all reduce to the same 4.03% T-bill plus a near-zero term. They are one input counted three times, so I gave them zero weight rather than let the auto-blend triple-count the spot rate. auto_blend is also unweighted because its inputs are already represented directly. The remaining disagreement is between 4.03% spot and the 3.00% ten-year survey. This is a horizon mismatch rather than a data problem, and the 3-year average should sit between them, closer to spot because the early years of the path are anchored near today's rate. No forward curve is supplied to this stage, so I do not claim a market-implied path. The 60/40 weighting is a judgment of where a gradual glide from 4.03% toward a roughly 3.0-3.25% neutral rate averages over 36 months.

**Signal alignment.** The composite of +0.24 is mostly an artefact of how bill returns accrue rather than a directional view. Momentum (+0.50), trend (+0.48) and mean reversion (-0.73) reflect three years of unusually high carry (4.45% trailing 3-year return). A T-bill does not mean-revert in price; its income resets with policy. The meaningful signals are macro. Monetary-policy alignment (+0.14) and inflation alignment (+0.06) say sticky inflation and a not-yet-easy policy stance support near-term bill income, which argues against cutting the estimate toward the survey too aggressively. Regime_fit (-0.28) says bill returns in past expansion months ran below the unconditional mean, consistent with rates drifting lower over a multi-year window. Together they support an estimate below spot but well above the 10-year consensus, which is where 3.62% sits. Fund-flow or positioning signals are not available to this pipeline, and I make no claim about them.

**Rationale.** I chose a custom blend of 60% yield_building_block (4.03%) and 40% survey_consensus (3.00%), giving 3.62%, inside the [3.00, 4.03] range. The governing IPS (config/ips.yaml) is ratified. The starting yield is the strongest single anchor for a bill, but the agent file is explicit that a 3-year CMA must average the expected path rather than quote today's yield. The ten-year SPF bill forecast supplies the direction and approximate destination of that path, and the 60/40 split reflects that the early part of the horizon stays near spot. The historical, regime and Black-Litterman candidates are excluded because each is the 4.03% spot rate plus a near-zero excess term, so they would repeat the spot input rather than add evidence. The 0.41pp shortfall versus the auto-blend's 3.78% comes from removing that duplication. This number matters well beyond cash. Under decision 38 it becomes the risk-free rate in every Sharpe ratio, so a lower cash CMA modestly raises every risky asset's implied Sharpe ratio and keeps cash from appearing strictly dominated. The negative historical Sharpe ratios in the stats table reflect exactly the mismatch that decision fixes. Cash's 0.62% volatility remains the universe floor, so variance-minimising methods will gravitate to it absent IPS floors; that follows from its risk, not from this CMA. A 3.62% nominal return minus sticky above-target inflation falls well short of the CPI + 3% objective, so a cash-heavy portfolio cannot meet the IPS. That is a true property of the asset, which earns its place as the funding and liquidity account and for optionality rather than return. Confidence is medium-high: the asset is near-mechanical, but the low-confidence regime call makes the policy path, and therefore the 3-year average, the main source of uncertainty.
