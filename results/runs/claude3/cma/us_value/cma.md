## Judgment

**Regime logic.** Stage 1 calls a fragile, late-cycle-adjacent expansion with sticky inflation, but only at low confidence (0.35) and with a 20-30% recession probability. Exhibit 4 tilts late-cycle environments toward valuation and regime-adjusted methods, so the cash-flow build carries the most weight. regime_adjusted keeps a moderate 20%: its 7.77% forward excess return after expansion months rests on 155 months and shrinks toward the unconditional premium (credibility 0.72), but the expansion label is uncertain. That uncertainty is also why I did not give it more. For value specifically, the regime mix is less hostile than for long-duration equity. Monetary policy is restrictive (-0.22) and inflation sticky, and value is the short-duration sleeve with a positive Rates sensitivity. Financials benefit from net interest margins and energy from inflation-linked commodity prices (considerations 1 and 2). That supports holding the estimate near the middle of the range rather than at the equilibrium floor. Nothing in the blend implies a negative rate sensitivity for this sleeve, which consideration 1 would treat as suspect.

**Valuation context.** Consideration 4 says dividends are a larger share of value's return, so inverse_gordon gets the largest weight (35%). Its components are clean: 1.78% dividends, 1.72% net buybacks from WRDS (rolled forward by IWD's price change), nominal GDP growth and the variance add-back, with zero valuation drift. That zero drift is a limitation, since there is no long valuation history for this index. implied_erp_cape (8.11%) uses IWD's trailing earnings yield, a proxy rather than a true cyclically adjusted figure. Trailing earnings for an energy- and financials-heavy index are cyclical, so it gets 15%. On consideration 3, I take the reading that a wide book-based value-growth spread partly reflects intangible capital that book value cannot measure. I therefore add no spread-reversion premium. None of the candidates is book-based, so the artefact does not enter the arithmetic; I simply decline to lean toward the top of the range on 'value is cheap' grounds. After a 23.7% one-year and 19.9% three-year run, starting yields have compressed. That argues against the backward-looking historical_erp (11.99%), which is held to 10%. The rally embedded in the 1990-2026 sample is exactly what the valuation methods correct for.

**Signal alignment.** The composite signal is mildly bullish (+0.24) but internally split. Relative momentum (+0.78) and 12-1 momentum (+0.49) are strong, while the 36-month mean-reversion signal is clearly negative (-0.58). Over a 3-year horizon, the mean-reversion reading is the more relevant one: momentum decays within about a year, while a stretched 36-month run tends to give back part of its gains over the next few years. The macro alignment signals are near zero, apart from financial conditions (+0.13) and regime fit (+0.17), both modestly supportive. The net picture confirms neither the 12% historical tail nor the 8% equilibrium floor. It supports a mid-range estimate a little below the auto-blend, which is where the blend lands.

**Rationale.** The final estimate is a custom blend of 9.51%, inside the 8.00-11.99% range and 0.39pp below the confidence-weighted auto-blend (9.90%). The adjustment is deliberately small; return forecasts are the least reliable input in the chain. The blend makes three departures from the auto-blend. First, it moves weight onto inverse_gordon (35%), because consideration 4 makes the payout build more reliable for value than for other sleeves. Second, it cuts historical_erp from 24% to 10%. That method extrapolates a 1990-2026 premium after a strong recent run that the mean-reversion signal flags as stretched, and it takes no account of starting valuations. Third, it keeps bl_equilibrium (8.00%) at 20% as an anchor. Its 5% ETF-AUM weight is only a proxy for market size, and the Russell style split means part of that weight double-counts names also held in us_growth (consideration 5). The BL figure also takes no view on the rate and commodity exposures, so it is not given more weight. The sector composition (consideration 2) makes this partly a view on bank margins and oil. Under sticky inflation and restrictive policy, that composition is a modest support, and it is reflected in the 20% regime_adjusted weight. No candidate is book-based, so the intangibles caveat (consideration 3) is handled by adding no value-spread reversion premium on top. Confidence is medium: dispersion is moderate at 3.99pp, the macro regime is held at low confidence, and the two highest-weighted valuation methods rely on a rolled-forward payout yield and a trailing earnings-yield proxy.
