The portfolio holds 0% in equity against a 60% benchmark weight (active equity -60.0%), so it will lag badly in any equity rally, and expansion is the regime where that opportunity cost is highest. Absolute market risk is small. The benchmark gap is the exposure that matters.; Purchasing-power erosion in short_treasuries (62.4%) and cash (37.6%). Both pay roughly the policy rate, so real return depends on inflation staying below that rate. The portfolio has no growth engine or inflation hedge, no real assets (active real assets +0.0%) and no equity.; Reinvestment-rate risk. Both holdings roll at prevailing short rates, so income falls quickly in a rate-cutting cycle. The 1.79% expected real return rests on the judged CMAs for cash and short Treasuries.; CMA sensitivity of the risk-adjusted return. The excess return over cash is only a few tenths of a percent, and the backtest Sharpe of 0.44 is lower than the 0.60 the proposer cites. A small error in the cash or short-Treasury CMA or in the risk-free rate swings the Sharpe a lot, and it says little about compensation for bearing risk.; Hidden single exposure. The two holdings are one short-rate exposure under two labels, so the 1.9 effective assets overstates diversification. A single rate path drives the whole outcome.
